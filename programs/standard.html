Admission

Entry requirements

A Bachelor's degree or the equivalent in biological/life sciences, chemistry, biomedical sciences/engineering, health science, or a related discipline.

  • Preference will be given to applicants having at least 1 year of relevant work experience.
  • If you are not a native speaker of English, and your Bachelor's degree or equivalent qualification is awarded by institutions where the medium of instruction is not English, you are expected to provide one of the following proficiency test results (taken in a single sitting within 2 years) to fulfil the University's minimum English language requirement for admission purposes:
    • An overall score of 80 out of 120 / 4.5 out of 6 (for tests taken on or after 21 Jan 2026) or above in the Test of English as a Foreign Language (TOEFL) Internet-based test
    • OR
    • An Overall Band score of 6.0 or above in the International English Language Testing System (IELTS) Academic module

    Remarks: TOEFL iBT Home Edition, the IELTS Online, IELTS Indicator test, and IELTS One Skill Retake results will not be considered for meeting the English language requirement for taught programmes.

Please refer to the Admission Requirements section for details.

Curriculum

Four pillars, one commercial mindset

The curriculum integrates the scientific, business, regulatory and ethical aspects of the biopharmaceutical landscape.

🧬

Advanced therapeutics

Biologics and protein engineering through to cell and gene therapies — how today's most complex medicines are designed and produced.

⚖️

Regulation & approval

Clinical trial design and the drug approval process: what regulators expect, and how evidence is built to satisfy them.

🔐

Patents & IP strategy

Patenting strategies that protect an asset, define freedom to operate and underpin every licensing conversation.

📈

Market entry & pricing

Commercial acumen — positioning, pricing and the route a therapy takes from approval to real patients.

Optional core 6 credits — choose one of two paths
ABCT5110 / ABCT5113 Industrial Attachment (6) or Research Project (6) Choose 1 of 2

Industrial Attachment — roughly 400 hours on placement. The department lists openings in a range of countries, and you may also source your own; either way, you interview and secure the position yourself.
Research Project — you approach a PolyU professor to supervise you, then spend six months on a hands-on project in their group. If you're aiming at a PhD, this gives genuine research experience to draw on — but it is research training only, not a PhD placement, with no commitment from the supervisor beyond the project.

Learning outcomes

What you'll be able to do

Graduates leave fluent in both halves of the industry conversation — able to hold their own with a research team in the morning and a commercial board in the afternoon.

  • Scientific proficiency in cutting-edge therapeutic modalities.
  • Commercial acumen covering market entry and pricing decisions.
  • Regulatory expertise spanning trials, approval and compliance.
  • Interdisciplinary collaboration across science, law and business teams.

Where graduates go

The skill set maps directly onto the roles biopharma companies struggle to fill:
